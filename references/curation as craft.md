# essay
caring about something enough to want to contribute 
## intro
We live in an age of relatively unlimited access to information. With the internet a few moves of a mouse or taps on a glass screen/keyboard you can get more information about "anything"
There is a freedom and power that comes with this access.
This makes it easy to slip into mindless consumption,
A lot of the time this feels like drinking from a fire hose, the thirst which was there at the beginning of the scrolling is never satisfied
The individual chooses if and when they want to consume it
## curation
With the power in the individuals hands, you get to be the one who chooses where to direct your attention,
this is where curation comes in 
it's about being intentional with the media chosen to consume
finding what you care about and going deeper into that
find the source
understanding how it has evolved over time 
document what you find, make a collage
letting it open you up to new ideas, thoughts and ways of being
## craft
consume then contribute
this is where craft comes in 
after getting to a source which resonates with you add your perspective to it
consume -> internalize -> edit -> give back
there is nothing that has not been already done before
all "new" things are just an addition or an update to things already done  
your perspective/lens on the past thing is the only new addition.
## power dynamics
The power has shifted from top down to bottom up
## top down
the medium(context) is limited -> radio/tv/newspaper/magazine/book
context constrained by the medium
the content and timing of release is chosen by the "powers" that be all you really are in the process in the end consumer
curation(external) -> consumption
## bottom up
the medium is relatively unlimited -> the internet  
the context is chosen by the creator 
# definitions
## curation  
the process of selecting, organizing, and managing items, content, or resources to present them in a meaningful and valuable way. It often involves careful selection, categorization, and arrangement to provide a curated collection that meets specific criteria or serves a particular purpose, such as in museums, galleries, libraries, or online content platforms. Curators typically aim to deliver a cohesive and engaging experience for their audience by curating items that are relevant, interesting, or significant.
### roots
 1. Latin 
    The word "curate" ultimately comes from the Latin word "curare," which means "to care for" or "to take care of." In ancient Rome, a "cura" referred to the care or management of something, such as property or responsibilities.
2. Old French
	The term passed into Old French as "curer," which meant "to cure" or "to take care of." It was used in the context of spiritual care and administration in the medieval Christian Church.
3. Middle English
	In Middle English, "curate" emerged as a noun referring to a clergyman who was responsible for the spiritual care and administration of a parish or congregation. This usage has strong ties to the Church of England and the Anglican tradition.
## craft
Craft refers to the skilled and creative practice of making objects or creating works of art using manual skills, often with an emphasis on traditional techniques and attention to detail. Crafts encompass a wide range of activities and materials, including but not limited to woodworking, pottery, weaving, metalworking, glassblowing, and more. Craftsmanship involves the application of specialized skills and knowledge to produce items that are functional, decorative, or both. Crafts are often associated with cultural traditions and heritage, and they can be seen as a form of artistic expression that values precision, quality, and the mastery of techniques.
### roots
1. Old English
   the word "cræft" (pronounced like "craft") was used to describe skill, power, or strength. It was a broad term that encompassed various forms of skill, from craftsmanship to cunning or expertise.
2. Middle English
   During the Middle English period, which spanned from the 11th to the 15th century, the term "craft" began to take on a more specific meaning related to skilled work, especially in trades and occupations. Guilds and trade associations played a significant role in defining and regulating various crafts during this time.
3. Renaissance and Early Modern English
   In the Renaissance and Early Modern English periods, "craft" continued to be associated with skilled manual labor and trades. Craftsmen were highly regarded for their expertise, and the term was commonly used to describe professions and trades that required specialized skills and knowledge.
4. Modern Usage
   In modern usage, "craft" still refers to skilled workmanship and the practice of creating objects or works of art using manual skills and traditional techniques. It encompasses a wide range of activities, from woodworking and pottery to weaving, metalworking, and more. The term has also extended to describe the art of brewing beer or making other artisanal products.
